# Vault

**Live:** <https://derlocke-ng.github.io/armory/vault/>

Vault is kiwi-framework's **vault** app, mounted here (`framework: 'vault'` in [`distribution.js`](../../distribution.js)): a password manager for logins, notes, cards, two-factor codes, SSH keys and certificate authorities, on every device you sign in on. A generator beside every password (random characters or a passphrase of words), a health report (weak, reused and year-old passwords; known breaches only if you turn that check on), imports from Bitwarden, KeePass, KeePassXC, the browsers, 1Password, LastPass, Dashlane and Proton Pass, and exports for them. SSH keys made here or brought in, an SSH key used as a certificate authority that signs user and host certificates, and an X.509 CA that issues server and client certificates (or signs a CSR) for your own services. TLS certificates in one step, like kiwi-server's ksslgen but in your browser: from your CA, self-signed, or a key and request for another CA; host and *.host together; saved as privkey.pem, cert.pem and fullchain.pem with the nginx lines; renewed in one tap and flagged in Health before they run out; a CA limited to your domains, or your existing kiwiCA brought in as it is, handed to your devices as PEM or DER with the steps for each system. ssh-keygen and openssl read what comes out.

Everything in it is sealed with a passphrase of its own, which even your account's password does not open: each entry is its own event on the relays, which see how many there are and when they change, nothing else. Authenticator opens the same vault and shows every entry with a 2FA code. A web page cannot fill in other sites' forms: Vault copies the username and password, and opens the site. Its code, browser suite and documentation live in the framework: [`apps/vault`](https://github.com/derlocke-ng/kiwi-framework/tree/main/apps/vault). This folder holds only what Armory lays over it (the favicon) and its catalog entry (`kiwi.manifest`).

`node test/e2e/vault.mjs` runs the framework's suite against Armory's own site, with Authenticator beside it.
